This week at a glance

An acute novel-transmission event dominates the briefing for week 20. Two longer-running concerns continue to require attention; one global threat has receded.

Bottom line: The Andes hantavirus cluster aboard the MV Hondius — 11 cases, 3 deaths, ~27% case-fatality, multi-country dispersal of contacts — is this week's leading signal because it is acute, novel in setting, and saturating both official channels and public attention. Mpox clade Ib remains a smoldering PHEIC with continued community transmission in five European countries. H5N1 stays on the long-watch list given persistent US dairy-cattle spread and a fresh fatal pediatric case abroad. Global public-health risk from the hantavirus event is currently assessed as low by WHO; pandemic potential is judged limited by independent virologists.

Why it matters this week

First known hantavirus outbreak on a cruise ship. Strong sequencing evidence of limited human-to-human transmission of Andes virus on board — the only hantavirus species with documented person-to-person spread. Passengers of 19 nationalities have now dispersed via military and government repatriation flights.

For policymakers

  • Coordinate cross-border contact tracing for repatriated passengers (incubation window up to ~6 weeks).
  • Brief ports, cruise lines and travel-health authorities on rodent-exclusion and onboard isolation protocols.
  • Avoid over-reaction: WHO assesses global public-health risk as low; no sustained transmission outside the ship.
  • Confirm domestic clinical alert pathways — many physicians have never seen HPS.

Why it matters this week

The overall African trend is declining since May 2025, but sustained community transmission of clade Ib continues in Austria, Belgium, Portugal, Spain and the UK, mostly in sexual networks of MSM. A WHO situation report (#65) and an ECDC weekly threats note both updated this month. A novel clade Ib/IIb recombinant has been flagged in WHO DON 2026-DON595 — worth monitoring.

For policymakers

  • Sustain targeted JYNNEOS / MVA-BN vaccine availability for at-risk groups; supply tightening in some markets.
  • Maintain non-stigmatizing messaging through community organizations; avoid travel restrictions.
  • Fund continued genomic surveillance — clade I/II recombinants are emerging.
  • Review reimbursement codes for outpatient tecovirimat where stockpiles permit.

Why it matters this week

The headline number is quiet, but the substrate is not. H5N1 continues to spread in US dairy cattle across 19 states, with 57 cumulative human cases (mostly farm workers). May 2026 saw the first confirmed human H5N5 infection (NEJM) and one new fatal pediatric H5N1 case in Bangladesh. No sustained human-to-human transmission. Pandemic-preparedness risk remains the highest among the three pathogens here.

For policymakers

  • Maintain PPE distribution and seroprevalence studies for dairy / poultry workers.
  • Accelerate H5 candidate vaccine procurement contracts (matched-strain stocks).
  • Mandate bulk-tank milk surveillance; FDA retail-milk monitoring remains the early-warning workhorse.
  • Pre-position diagnostics & antivirals; review cross-border alerts for human clusters.

Methodology

The pathogen-of-the-week is selected by combining three signal classes:

  • Official surveillance signal — WHO Disease Outbreak News, ECDC Communicable Disease Threats Report, CDC Health Alert Network and outbreak listings, national health-agency advisories.
  • Severity signal — case count, case-fatality ratio, geographic dispersion, transmissibility (R, secondary attack rate), clinical severity, treatment availability.
  • Attention signal — volume and tier of news coverage (Google News / AP / Reuters / Nature / NEJM / national broadcasters), Wikipedia page-view trend, social-media mention volume where APIs allow.

Each pathogen is scored 0–10 on five dimensions (Severity, Novelty, Public attention, Policy urgency, Pandemic potential). The lead story has the highest composite when weighted toward acute novelty and attention; the watch-list slot privileges pandemic potential over current case count.
