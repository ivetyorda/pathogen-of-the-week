Why this matters

The PHEIC declared on 16 May has escalated sharply. In nine days (15–24 May), suspected cases surged from ~246 to 650+, deaths nearly doubled from 80 to 160+, and confirmed cases more than doubled (34 → 64). The US Title 42 entry suspension on 18 May marks the first deployment of this authority since COVID-19 and signals maximal alarm. Dr. Peter Stafford, a US physician working in Bunia since 2023, was confirmed positive on 18 May and medevaced to Germany with six high-risk-contact Americans. The same day the CDC confirmed an independent second American case in Uganda. Critical gaps remain: no approved Bundibugyo-specific therapeutics or vaccines, weak contact-tracing in insecurity-affected zones, and four healthcare worker deaths in 72 hours at Mongbwalu General Referral Hospital signal catastrophic IPC failures. WHO and Africa CDC are convening R&D Blueprint expert review of candidate monoclonal antibodies, remdesivir, and BVD vaccine candidates for emergency-use clinical trials.

Policy bullets

  • Activate cross-border surveillance and exit screening between DRC, Uganda, Rwanda, South Sudan, CAR and Kenya; brief national EOCs on BVD-specific clinical case definitions and rapid PCR panels that distinguish BVD from Zaire ebolavirus.
  • Establish emergency-use clinical-trial protocols in DRC and Uganda for investigational BVD monoclonal antibodies, remdesivir (IV and oral), and pre-clinical BVD vaccine candidates; existing Zaire-targeted countermeasures are not assumed cross-protective.
  • Scale isolation and ETC capacity in Bunia, Mongbwalu and Kampala; pre-position PPE and surge medevac air-ambulance contracts; brief receiving hospitals (Hamburg, London Royal Free, NIH-Bethesda, others) on BVD-specific case management and staff protections.
  • Communicate early and clearly: BVD spreads through direct contact with body fluids of symptomatic patients, not through airborne or casual contact. Counter stigma against travellers and Congolese diaspora; emphasize Title 42 as a screening measure, not a trade ban.
  • Stand up molecular epidemiology team to sequence isolates from Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Kampala and any exported cases; identify transmission chains and zoonotic reservoir(s) to guide containment.

Why this matters

The MV Hondius docked at Rotterdam on 18 May 2026. The Dutch RIVM reported no symptomatic disembarking; the remaining 25 crew and 2 medical staff entered quarantine and are undergoing clinical monitoring. Andes virus is the only hantavirus with documented human-to-human transmission, and the incubation window extends to ~6 weeks. Repatriated passengers and crew are being followed in 19 home countries. WHO still assesses global public-health risk as low, but operational burden on border health services, receiving countries' contact-tracing systems, and international clinical-alert pathways is high. No new symptomatic cases have been reported among disembarking personnel or early contacts as of 24 May.

Policy bullets

  • Sustain cross-border contact tracing for repatriated passengers and crew across 19 countries; incubation window extends up to ~6 weeks; median onset 14–17 days.
  • Support the Dutch RIVM disembarkation and disinfection protocol; provide secondary clinical-alert pathways for any contact developing fever, cough or respiratory symptoms.
  • Codify cruise-line protocols: rodent-exclusion, onboard isolation, cabin decontamination, passenger-screening for exposure history on embarkation.
  • Ensure domestic clinical-alert pathways are in place — many physicians have not seen hantavirus pulmonary syndrome; distribute clinical recognition materials to respiratory and ICU clinicians.

Why this matters

ECDC's monthly mpox surveillance shows sustained clade I transmission in Europe, with 80 clade I cases reported across 10 EU/EEA countries in March 2026 (Spain leading with 23 cases), 78 in February and 89 in January — demonstrating persistent endemic circulation at moderate levels. Just 23% of reported clade I cases had completed a two-dose JYNNEOS / MVA-BN course, indicating persistent vaccination gaps despite two years of availability. Risk to MSM remains moderate; risk to general population remains low. The Bundibugyo ebolavirus outbreak and hantavirus cruise-ship cluster add operational burden on national health authorities in DRC and cross-border zones. A novel clade Ib/IIb recombinant remains flagged (WHO DON 2026-DON595) but not detected in recent surveillance.

Policy bullets

  • Sustain targeted JYNNEOS / MVA-BN vaccine availability for at-risk MSM groups; address coverage gap — 77% of reported clade I cases were unvaccinated or partially vaccinated.
  • Maintain non-stigmatizing messaging through community organizations and sexual-health clinics; avoid travel restrictions for clade Ib.
  • Fund continued genomic surveillance — clade I/II recombinants are emerging; ECDC's new monthly reporting will track variants in real time.
  • Review reimbursement codes for outpatient tecovirimat where stockpiles permit; assess clinical efficacy data from ongoing observational cohorts.

Methodology

Each week, we scan WHO Disease Outbreak News, ECDC Communicable Disease Threat Reports, CDC HAN advisories, and major news outlets (Reuters, AP, BBC, Nature, NEJM, CIDRAP, national broadcasters) for emerging infectious disease signals.

We select the top 3 pathogens and assign each a profile:

  • ACUTE/LEAD: High novelty, high attention, rapid escalation, policy-critical.
  • ACUTE: Active investigation, cross-border spread, public health response ongoing.
  • SMOLDERING: Persistent transmission, moderate severity, policy gaps.
  • WATCH: High pandemic potential, emerging drug resistance, zoonotic risk.

Each pathogen is scored 0–10 on five dimensions:

  • Severity: Case fatality rate, hospitalization burden, organ damage.
  • Novelty: First appearance, emergence in unexpected populations, new variant.
  • Attention: Media coverage intensity, public concern, health worker alarm.
  • Policy urgency: Regulatory action needed, treatment/vaccine gaps, border risk.
  • Pandemic potential: Basic reproduction number, transmission mode, geographic spread.
