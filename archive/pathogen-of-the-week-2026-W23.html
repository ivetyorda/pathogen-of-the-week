Why it matters this week

The outbreak has shifted from explosive growth into a high-intensity stabilisation phase. DRC now reports 282 confirmed cases across 17 health zones (Ituri 264, North Kivu 15, South Kivu 3), plus 220 suspected under investigation, with 42 confirmed deaths. Five people — including four nurses — have recovered, the first clear evidence that survival with supportive care is achievable. WHO and Africa CDC R&D Blueprint have shortlisted candidate countermeasures for emergency-use clinical trials: monoclonal antibodies MBP 134 and maftivimab, remdesivir (IV/oral), oral obeldesivir targeted for post-exposure prophylaxis, and two BVD-specific vaccine candidates. Title 42 entry restrictions remain in force. Nosocomial transmission continues to drive spread; IPC gaps remain the critical operational bottleneck.

For policymakers

  • Accelerate WHO/Africa CDC R&D Blueprint emergency-use clinical-trial protocols for MBP 134, maftivimab and remdesivir in DRC and Uganda; coordinate regulatory fast-track pathways so enrolment can begin within days, not weeks.
  • Operationalise obeldesivir post-exposure prophylaxis for high-risk healthcare contacts; recovery data from the five confirmed survivors should inform supportive-care protocols for treatment centres in Bunia, Mongbwalu and Kampala.
  • Maintain cross-border exit screening between DRC, Uganda, Rwanda, South Sudan, CAR and Kenya; sustain Title 42 screening at US ports of entry while avoiding broad travel-restriction language that depresses voluntary healthcare-seeking.
  • Prioritise IPC surge in all 17 affected health zones; emergency PPE pre-positioning and rapid IPC training for frontline health workers must be resourced separately from case-isolation funding.

Why it matters this week

With 1,983 confirmed cases across 40 US jurisdictions as of 28 May 2026, the US is tracking closely to its full-year 2025 total of 2,288 — with seven months of 2026 remaining. Thirty new outbreaks have been identified in 2026 alone, with 93% of cases outbreak-associated. Epidemiologists have formally flagged that US measles elimination status — held since 2000 — is at risk. Nine cases are among international visitors, confirming ongoing importation risk. The core driver is declining MMR vaccination coverage in select communities. A post-outbreak vaccination spike has been documented in some states, but structural coverage gaps persist.

For policymakers

  • Immediately assess MMR vaccination coverage at county level in all 40 affected jurisdictions; identify sub-80% pockets for targeted catch-up campaigns — those communities are the outbreak accelerants.
  • Activate state emergency vaccination authority where outbreaks are active: school-exemption review, mobile vaccination teams, and community partnerships with faith and school networks in hesitant communities.
  • Formally communicate the elimination-status risk threshold to political leadership — the US could lose WHO measles-elimination certification if current trends continue through 2026.
  • Fund real-time outbreak surveillance infrastructure: a 93% outbreak-association rate means most cases are preventable with faster ring-vaccination response at outbreak detection.

Why it matters this week

The US multistate H5N1 outbreak continues with 71 human infections confirmed since 2024, 2 deaths, and cases spanning 13 states. The most concerning signal: 3 of those 71 cases have no identified animal-exposure source — raising the possibility of missed exposures, environmental transmission, or limited undetected human-to-human spread. No sustained H2H transmission has been documented. A first human H5N5 infection was reported in NEJM this week — a novel variant requiring urgent monitoring. Globally, WHO tracked 13 avian/swine influenza human cases in Q1 2026, including a fatal H5N1 case in a child in Bangladesh. Scientists publicly warn H5N1 could spark a pandemic in 2026.

For policymakers

  • Urgently investigate all 3 unknown-exposure US H5N1 cases with full environmental and contact-tracing workups; any evidence of human-to-human transmission must trigger immediate escalation to pandemic preparedness activation.
  • Strengthen surveillance in dairy-farm workers beyond symptomatic testing — serological screening is needed to detect asymptomatic cases that are likely being missed given the unknown-exposure detections.
  • Accelerate H5N1 pandemic vaccine candidate production and pre-positioning; WHO and HHS should review current CVV stock against circulating H5 clade 2.3.4.4b strains and update if antigenic drift is detected.
  • Add H5N5 to WHO global surveillance net — ensure national reference labs have PCR panels that distinguish H5 subtypes.

Methodology

The pathogen-of-the-week is selected by combining three signal classes:

  • Official surveillance signal — WHO Disease Outbreak News, Africa CDC weekly event reports, ECDC Communicable Disease Threats Report, US CDC Health Alert Network and outbreak listings, UKHSA notifiable-disease updates, Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC) advisories, and other national health-agency communications.
  • Severity signal — case count, case-fatality ratio, geographic dispersion, transmissibility (R, secondary attack rate), clinical severity, treatment availability.
  • Attention signal — volume and tier of news coverage (AP / Reuters / Nature / NEJM / national broadcasters), Wikipedia page-view trend, social-media mention volume where APIs allow.

Each pathogen is scored 0–10 on five dimensions (Severity, Novelty, Public attention, Policy urgency, Pandemic potential). The lead story has the highest composite when weighted toward acute novelty and attention; the watch-list slot privileges pandemic potential over current case count.
